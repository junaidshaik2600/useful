### Your career progression

* QA Analyst

    Auditing, policies, scorecards, accuracy and quality metrics.

* Senior QA Analyst

    Advanced auditing, calibration, RCA, analytics and reporting.

* QA Specialist / SME

    Policy expertise, complex cases, process improvement and coaching.

* Quality Lead

    Team performance, coaching, quality targets and stakeholder management.

* QA Manager

    Quality strategy, governance, reporting, people management and business outcomes.

* Quality Program Manager

    Multiple teams, large-scale quality programmes and strategic improvements.

### My recommended learning priority

Don't try to learn everything simultaneously. Follow this sequence:

* QA fundamentals

* Trust & Safety quality

* Quality metrics and statistics

* Advanced Excel and Google Sheets

* RCA and process improvement

* SQL

* Power BI

* Communication and coaching

* Team leadership

* Advanced QA management

Important: You don't need to become an automation engineer or full-stack developer to become a QA Manager in your current career direction. Data analysis, quality management, policy expertise and people leadership should be your primary strengths.

Your ultimate objective: Become someone who can identify quality problems, explain why they happen, prove them using data, recommend improvements and lead a team to prevent them from happening again.
